Figure 6: Multimodal Sensory Integration in the Male Courtship Circuit
======================================================================

Central question
----------------
Male courtship in Drosophila requires integrating multiple sensory cues:
pheromones (3 olfactory channels: DA1, VA1v, VA1d), song (auditory),
visual motion, and contact chemosensation (ppk23, ppk25). How does the
male CNS connectome wire these 7 sensory channels to converge on
courtship decision neurons -- and does this wiring produce genuine
nonlinear integration?


Act 1: The wiring (Panels A-C)
------------------------------
Panel A  Circuit schematic
  7 modalities -> 2nd-order relays -> 3rd-order command neurons.
  Sets the stage: what is the circuit?

Panel B  Composition heatmap
  "Who gets what?" Each target neuron's modality input profile,
  column-normalized to reveal relative targeting patterns independent
  of absolute strength.

Panel C  Valence heatmap
  "With what sign?" Same structure, split into excitatory vs.
  inhibitory contributions. Modalities don't just converge -- they
  converge with distinct E/I signatures.


Act 2: Quantifying multimodality (Panels D-F)
----------------------------------------------
Panel D  Entropy / multimodality
  Most targets are genuinely multimodal (high Shannon entropy), not
  dominated by a single sense. Strength vs. entropy separates
  strong-but-narrow from weak-but-broad neurons.

Panel E  Modality correlations
  Which modality pairs co-target the same neurons? Reveals modality
  groupings (the 3 olfactory channels cluster; contact channels
  form a separate group).

Panel F  Path diversity
  Some modalities reach targets via a few dominant paths; others
  distribute signal across many parallel routes. This affects
  robustness and information capacity.


Act 3: Where and how integration happens (Panels G-H)
-----------------------------------------------------
Panel G  Shared intermediates
  Identifies hidden integration hubs -- 2nd-order neurons that appear
  in strongest paths from multiple modalities. These are the circuit's
  multimodal crossroads.

Panel H  Spotlight integrators
  Deep dive into specific hub neurons. Radar plots showing their
  modality input fingerprint (excitatory and inhibitory overlaid).


Act 4: Target-specific differences (Panels I-J)
------------------------------------------------
Panel I  aSP-f vs. aSP-g
  Same circuit architecture, different integration profiles. Paired
  comparisons reveal which modalities preferentially target one
  population over the other.

Panel J  Valence patterns
  Different modalities reach the same target via different
  neurotransmitter sequences (e.g., E->I->E vs. E->E->E). The sign
  of the path, not just its existence, matters for computation.


Act 5: Integration deepens with circuit depth (Panel K)
-------------------------------------------------------
Panel K  Layer-by-layer analysis
  2nd-order neurons are modality-specific; 3rd/4th-order neurons are
  increasingly multimodal. Convergence builds progressively across
  circuit layers.


Act 6: From structure to function -- combinatorial signal flow (NEW)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Everything above describes the wiring. The combinatorial signal flow
analysis asks: does this wiring actually produce nonlinear computation?

Method:
  - Propagate signal through the real signed connectome (excitatory/
    inhibitory weights, neurotransmitter identity)
  - Test all 127 combinations of the 7 channels (singles, pairs,
    triplets, ..., all 7 together)
  - Compute interaction scores:
      R(i,j) = A(i+j) - A(i) - A(j)
    Positive = synergy (super-additive)
    Negative = opposition (sub-additive)
    Zero = independence (linear summation)

Panel L  Synergistic pairs
  7x7 interaction heatmaps showing pairwise synergy scores averaged
  across target neurons in each group. Identifies which modality
  combinations are super-additive: the circuit is wired for specific
  multimodal conjunctions, not generic convergence.
  Source: interaction_heatmap_{tg}.pdf, summary_interaction_heatmap.pdf
  Key question: which sensory combinations amplify each other?

Panel M  Opposing pairs
  Same interaction framework, but focused on negative R_ij values
  (sub-additive combinations). Forest plots rank all 21 modality
  pairs by interaction strength, separating synergistic from opposing.
  Opposing pairs reveal competitive inhibition between channels via
  shared inhibitory interneurons (connects back to Panel C's E/I
  valence and Panel G's shared intermediates).
  Source: interaction_forest.pdf, interaction_forest_clustered.pdf
  Key question: which sensory channels compete?

Panel N  Target-group-specific interaction profiles
  The interaction structure differs fundamentally between target
  groups. Per-neuron interaction profile heatmaps (rows = target
  neurons, columns = modality pairs, color = R_ij) reveal that
  aSP-f, aSP-g, and PPN1/vAB3 downstream neurons compute different
  functions over the same sensory inputs. Clustering targets by
  their interaction profiles groups them differently than clustering
  by raw strength (Panel B) -- neurons that receive the same
  modalities may integrate them in opposite ways.
  Source: interaction_profile_{tg}.pdf, target_clustering.pdf,
         activation_profiles.pdf
  Key question: do different targets compute different things?

Panel O  Robustness and sensitivity
  Results hold across activation functions (ReLU, sigmoid, leaky
  ReLU), normalization schemes (post, pre, raw), and propagation
  depths (2-5 hops). Sensitivity grid shows that the qualitative
  interaction landscape is a property of the wiring, not an artifact
  of the model. Sustained vs. pulse input produces similar patterns.
  Source: sensitivity_grid.pdf, plots_sustained/
  Key question: are these findings robust?


Narrative flow across all panels
--------------------------------
The figure builds a layered argument:

  Structure (A-C)
    "Here is the multimodal courtship circuit and its E/I architecture"
        |
        v
  Quantification (D-F)
    "Most targets are genuinely multimodal, with structured modality
     groupings and diverse routing strategies"
        |
        v
  Mechanism (G-H)
    "Integration happens at specific hub neurons that sit at the
     crossroads of multiple modalities"
        |
        v
  Specificity (I-J)
    "Different target populations and different neurotransmitter
     sequences create distinct integration profiles"
        |
        v
  Depth (K)
    "Integration builds progressively across circuit layers --
     early layers are modality-specific, deep layers are multimodal"
        |
        v
  Function (L-O)   <-- the punchline
    "The wiring doesn't just converge -- it computes. Specific
     modality pairs are synergistic (L), others are opposing (M),
     and the interaction landscape is target-specific (N). These
     are robust properties of the connectome (O)."

The final message: the male courtship circuit is not a passive
funnel that pools sensory information. It is a structured
computational device where specific combinations of cues are
amplified, others are suppressed, and different output populations
extract different multimodal features from the same sensory input
space.
