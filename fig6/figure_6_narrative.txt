Figure 6: Multimodal Sensory Integration in the Male Courtship Circuit
======================================================================

Central question
----------------
Male courtship in Drosophila requires integrating multiple sensory cues:
pheromones (3 olfactory channels: DA1, VA1v, VA1d), song (auditory),
visual motion, and contact chemosensation (ppk23, ppk25). How does the
male CNS connectome wire these 7 sensory channels to converge on
courtship decision neurons -- and does this wiring produce genuine
nonlinear integration?


Act 1: The wiring (Panels A-C)
------------------------------
Panel A  Circuit schematic
  7 modalities -> 2nd-order relays -> 3rd-order command neurons.
  Sets the stage: what is the circuit?

Panel B  Composition heatmap
  "Who gets what?" Each target neuron's modality input profile,
  column-normalized to reveal relative targeting patterns independent
  of absolute strength.

Panel C  Valence heatmap
  "With what sign?" Same structure, split into excitatory vs.
  inhibitory contributions. Modalities don't just converge -- they
  converge with distinct E/I signatures.


Act 2: Quantifying multimodality (Panels D-F)
----------------------------------------------
Panel D  Entropy / multimodality
  Most targets are genuinely multimodal (high Shannon entropy), not
  dominated by a single sense. Strength vs. entropy separates
  strong-but-narrow from weak-but-broad neurons.

Panel E  Modality correlations
  Which modality pairs co-target the same neurons? Reveals modality
  groupings (the 3 olfactory channels cluster; contact channels
  form a separate group).

Panel F  Path diversity
  Some modalities reach targets via a few dominant paths; others
  distribute signal across many parallel routes. This affects
  robustness and information capacity.


Act 3: Where and how integration happens (Panels G-H)
-----------------------------------------------------
Panel G  Shared intermediates
  Identifies hidden integration hubs -- 2nd-order neurons that appear
  in strongest paths from multiple modalities. These are the circuit's
  multimodal crossroads.

Panel H  Spotlight integrators
  Deep dive into specific hub neurons. Radar plots showing their
  modality input fingerprint (excitatory and inhibitory overlaid).


Act 4: Target-specific differences (Panels I-J)
------------------------------------------------
Panel I  aSP-f vs. aSP-g
  Same circuit architecture, different integration profiles. Paired
  comparisons reveal which modalities preferentially target one
  population over the other.

Panel J  Valence patterns
  Different modalities reach the same target via different
  neurotransmitter sequences (e.g., E->I->E vs. E->E->E). The sign
  of the path, not just its existence, matters for computation.


Act 5: Integration deepens with circuit depth (Panel K)
-------------------------------------------------------
Panel K  Layer-by-layer analysis
  2nd-order neurons are modality-specific; 3rd/4th-order neurons are
  increasingly multimodal. Convergence builds progressively across
  circuit layers.


Act 6: From structure to function -- combinatorial signal flow (NEW)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Everything above describes the wiring. The combinatorial signal flow
analysis asks: does this wiring actually produce nonlinear computation?

Method:
  - Propagate signal through the real signed connectome (excitatory/
    inhibitory weights, neurotransmitter identity)
  - Test all 127 combinations of the 7 channels (singles, pairs,
    triplets, ..., all 7 together)
  - Compute interaction scores:
      R(i,j) = A(i+j) - A(i) - A(j)
    Positive = synergy (super-additive)
    Negative = opposition (sub-additive)
    Zero = independence (linear summation)

Key story points:
  1. Synergistic pairs -- specific modality combinations are
     super-additive at specific targets. The circuit is wired for
     particular multimodal conjunctions.

  2. Opposing pairs -- some combinations are sub-additive, suggesting
     competitive inhibition between channels via shared inhibitory
     interneurons.

  3. Target-group specificity -- the interaction structure differs
     between aSP-f, aSP-g, and PPN1/vAB3 targets. Different targets
     compute different functions over the same sensory inputs.

  4. Robustness -- results hold across activation functions (ReLU,
     sigmoid, leaky ReLU), normalization schemes, and propagation
     depths (sensitivity analysis).

  5. Sustained vs. pulse -- continuous stimulation (modeling persistent
     pheromone exposure) vs. transient input produces qualitatively
     similar but quantitatively different interaction landscapes.
