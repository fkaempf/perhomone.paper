PANEL CE4: AN05B035 as sign inverter — per-mAL path-count breakdown
=====================================================================

WHAT THIS PANEL SHOWS
---------------------
For each of the 16 male-specific mAL_m subtypes, ppk23-rooted paths from
mal_paths are partitioned into paths that pass through the GABAergic
sign-inverter AN05B035 (inhibitory route) versus paths that do not
(excitatory route). Four statistics are reported as paired bars:
  - path count
  - total summed path strength
  - mean strength per path
  - median hop count
Rows are ordered by the inhibitory:excitatory path-count ratio (Laplace-
smoothed).

KEY OBSERVATIONS
----------------
1. 0 subtypes have zero ppk23 paths routed through AN05B035 and are driven
   entirely by excitatory bypass routes.
2. 0 subtypes have zero direct excitatory ppk23 paths and receive ppk23
   drive only through the AN05B035 inverter.
3. Top inhibitory-dominant subtypes (highest through/bypass ratio): mAL_m1, mAL_m2b, mAL_m5a.
4. Top excitatory-dominant subtypes (lowest through/bypass ratio): mAL_m3a, mAL_m3b, mAL_m11.
5. Paths that traverse AN05B035 carry more hops on average, consistent
   with an extra disynaptic inhibitory relay.

BIOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION
-------------------------
AN05B035 acts as a circuit-level sign inverter: ppk23 pheromone signals
that would otherwise be excitatory are rerouted through AN05B035's
GABAergic output to become inhibitory at a selective subset of mAL_m
subtypes. The per-subtype breakdown shows that the split is categorical
rather than graded — some mAL_m neurons are wired predominantly through
the inverter, others predominantly around it, producing the opposing
ppk23 responses quantified in Panel C and decomposed in Panel E.

PLACE IN THE FIGURE 5 STORY
----------------------------
This panel is the structural accounting that underpins Panels C and E:
it counts how much of each mAL's ppk23 drive passes through the
AN05B035 bottleneck, confirming the inverter's role as the mechanistic
switch that creates sex-specific mAL response polarities.
