FIGURE 5 (MALE-SPECIFIC SUBSET): The Male-Specific mAL Population Code
=========================================================================

NOTE ON SUBSET
--------------
This version restricts analysis to the 16 male-specific mAL subtypes
(mAL_m1 through mAL_m11, including mAL_m2a/b, mAL_m3a/b/c, mAL_m5a/b/c).
Shared subtypes (present in both sexes, e.g. mAL4F, mAL1A) are excluded.
This focuses the analysis on the sexually dimorphic population that is
unique to males and most directly linked to courtship regulation.


CENTRAL THESIS
--------------
The male-specific mAL subtypes are not a uniform courtship gate. Even
within the male-specific subset alone, there is substantial functional
diversity: subtypes differ in their sensory input profiles, pheromone
channel preferences, E/I balance, lateral interactions, and P1 output
specificity. This diversity enables the male-specific population to
encode pheromone encounter identity and deliver channel-specific gating
of P1 courtship command neurons.


ENCOUNTER SCENARIOS
-------------------
Three biologically grounded encounter types (lone male contact excluded
because male pheromone contact always co-occurs with cVA volatile):

  1. Female encounter:  ppk23 + ppk25     (contact with female)
  2. cVA + male:        DA1 + ppk23       (volatile + male contact)
  3. cVA + female:      DA1 + ppk23 + ppk25 (volatile + female contact)

Key contrasts:
  - cVA+female vs cVA+male:  sex discrimination when cVA is present
  - cVA+female vs Female:    effect of adding volatile context to female contact
  - cVA+male vs Female:      male contact+volatile vs female contact alone

PANEL-BY-PANEL GUIDE
====================

A. Sensory Input Profiles (heatmap)
-----------------------------------
PURPOSE: Establish that even within the male-specific subset, mAL_m
subtypes have diverse, non-uniform input profiles across 7 sensory
channels. Hierarchical clustering reveals functional subgroups:
ppk23-biased, ppk25-biased, olfactory-responsive, and multimodal
subtypes -- all within the 16 male-specific types.

WHY THIS PANEL MATTERS: Demonstrates that male-specific mAL is not
monolithic. These 16 subtypes tile sensory space differently, laying
the groundwork for population-level encoding.


B. AN09B017 Relay Selectivity Spectrum
--------------------------------------
PURPOSE: Explain HOW input diversity is created. The ascending neuron
family AN09B017 (variants a-g) tiles a selectivity spectrum from ppk23-
biased to ppk25-biased. AN09B017b receives 12.6x more ppk23 than ppk25
input; AN09B017g receives 16x more ppk25 than ppk23. This relay layer
decomposes a mixed pheromone signal into channel-specific streams before
it reaches the male-specific mAL population.

WHY THIS PANEL MATTERS: The relay layer is the mechanistic origin of
mAL_m diversity. Channel separation begins upstream of mAL.


K. AN09B017 Output Specificity (heatmap)
-----------------------------------------
PURPOSE: Show that relay selectivity is paired with output specificity
to male-specific mAL targets. AN09B017 variants target different
mAL_m subtypes, completing the labeled-line mapping: ppk23 -> AN09B017b
-> specific mAL_m subtypes. Shannon entropy quantifies targeting breadth.

WHY THIS PANEL MATTERS: With shared types removed, the relay-to-target
mapping focuses on the sexually dimorphic computation. Together with B,
this shows that pheromone streams are specifically routed to the male-
specific population.


C. ppk23 Sign Reversal (paired bars + scatter)
-----------------------------------------------
PURPOSE: Show the key computational surprise within the male-specific
population. The same pheromone receptor (ppk23, male contact) produces
OPPOSITE effects at different mAL_m subtypes. Some are excited; others
are inhibited. This is mediated by the GABAergic interneuron AN05B035,
which intercepts ppk23 signals and delivers inhibition to specific
mAL_m targets.

WHY THIS PANEL MATTERS: Sign reversal transforms the male-specific
population from a simple relay into a computing element. A single
stimulus (male contact) simultaneously excites and inhibits different
members of the same population.


E. Valence Decomposition (E/I balance)
---------------------------------------
PURPOSE: Decompose the net responses from Panel C into excitatory and
inhibitory components. Every mAL_m subtype receives BOTH excitatory and
inhibitory paths from ppk23 and ppk25. The net response is the balance
of these opposing forces. ppk25 is almost universally excitatory;
ppk23 has a strong inhibitory component via AN05B035.

WHY THIS PANEL MATTERS: Shows the mechanistic substrate for sign reversal
within the male-specific population. Small changes in E/I balance could
flip a subtype's response polarity.


G. Lateral Inhibition Architecture
-----------------------------------
PURPOSE: Show that mAL_m subtypes compete through lateral GABAergic
inhibition within the male-specific population. mAL_m1 is the dominant
inhibitory hub, followed by mAL_m8. This competitive architecture
sharpens the population code by suppressing weakly activated subtypes.

WHY THIS PANEL MATTERS: With shared types excluded, the lateral
inhibition network is purely male-specific. Competition creates
winner-take-all dynamics that sharpen population representations.


F/F'. Population Encounter Signatures (parallel coordinates)
------------------------------------------------------------
PURPOSE: Show the functional payoff. Under 3 encounter scenarios
(female, cVA+male, cVA+female), the male-specific mAL population
produces DISTINCT activation patterns. F uses path-based strengths;
F' uses the signal flow model.

Difference profiles show:
  - cVA+female vs cVA+male:  which mAL_m subtypes carry the sex-
    discrimination signal when volatile context is present
  - cVA+female vs Female:    which mAL_m subtypes respond to adding cVA
  - cVA+male vs Female:      male-with-volatile vs female contrast

WHY THIS PANEL MATTERS: The male-specific population alone is sufficient
to discriminate encounter type. Diversity, relay routing, sign reversal,
E/I balance, and lateral competition combine to produce a population
code for encounter discrimination even without shared subtypes.


L/L'. ppk Selectivity Propagation to mAL and P1
-------------------------------------------------
PURPOSE: Show that pheromone selectivity extends beyond mAL_m subtypes
to the P1 courtship command neurons using the signal flow model.

  X-axis = ppk23 alone (signal flow net input)
  Y-axis = ppk23 + ppk25 coactivation (signal flow net input)

Points above the diagonal gain drive from ppk25 addition (female contact
enhancement). The male-specific subtypes that are most enhanced by ppk25
addition propagate this selectivity downstream to P1.

WHY THIS PANEL MATTERS: Connects the male-specific population code to
downstream behavioral output. P1 subtypes receive non-uniform pheromone
drive routed through the male-specific mAL population.


D/D'. mAL-to-P1 Output Specificity
------------------------------------
PURPOSE: Show that male-specific mAL subtypes do not uniformly inhibit
all P1 neurons. D shows the biclustered heatmap revealing block-diagonal
structure; D' shows total drive per P1 subtype. Within the male-specific
population, mAL_m8 is the strongest P1 inhibitor, followed by mAL_m1.
Strikingly, mAL_m3b and mAL_m3a are net EXCITATORY to P1.

WHY THIS PANEL MATTERS: Block-diagonal structure means sensory streams
maintain labeled-line separation through to motor output, even when
considering only the male-specific population.


I. Model Convergence (path-based vs signal flow)
-------------------------------------------------
PURPOSE: Validate predictions using two independent computational
approaches on the male-specific subset. Path-based and signal flow
models strongly agree. Scenario discriminability analysis identifies
which mAL_m subtypes shift most across the 3 encounter scenarios.

WHY THIS PANEL MATTERS: Convergence of two independent models on the
male-specific subset confirms that the population code is robust
and not an artifact of including shared types.


EXAMPLE NEURONS TO HIGHLIGHT
=============================

All examples are male-specific (mAL_m*) subtypes:

1. mAL_m1 (GABAergic, male-specific)
   THE CLASSICAL COURTSHIP GATE
   - The population's master regulator
   - Strongest lateral inhibition hub (0.76 normalized output)
   - Second strongest P1 inhibitor (-0.70)
   - Strongly ppk23-excited: activated by contact pheromone
   - Story: "Contact pheromone activates mAL_m1, which suppresses rival
     mAL_m subtypes through lateral inhibition AND directly inhibits P1
     courtship neurons. This is the classical courtship-suppressing
     mAL function -- but it's just one subtype in a diverse population."

2. mAL_m3b (unclear NT, male-specific)
   THE SURPRISE EXCITER
   - One of only two mAL_m types with net EXCITATORY P1 output (+0.12)
   - Challenges the dogma that mAL only inhibits courtship
   - High signal flow activation, moderate scenario discriminability
   - Story: "Not all mAL-to-P1 transmission is inhibitory. mAL_m3b
     is a pro-courtship element within a predominantly inhibitory
     population, suggesting mAL output can PROMOTE specific courtship
     subroutines under certain sensory combinations."

3. mAL_m3c (GABAergic, male-specific)
   THE ENCOUNTER ENCODER
   - Most scenario-discriminating subtype across all 3 encounters
   - Strongest ppk23 excitation (0.11) and highest signal flow response
   - Its activation shifts most between female, cVA+male, cVA+female
   - Story: "mAL_m3c is the most context-sensitive subtype. Its
     activation best distinguishes who the fly is touching and whether
     volatile pheromone is present, making it a candidate encoder of
     encounter identity."

4. mAL_m8 (GABAergic, male-specific)
   THE DOMINANT P1 SUPPRESSOR
   - Strongest P1 inhibitor in the male-specific population (-0.82)
   - Second strongest lateral inhibition hub (0.39)
   - Complements mAL_m1 as a parallel inhibitory controller
   - Story: "mAL_m8 delivers the strongest inhibition to P1 courtship
     neurons in the entire male-specific population. While mAL_m1
     dominates lateral competition, mAL_m8 dominates the output to P1,
     suggesting a division of labor within the inhibitory architecture."

5. AN09B017b + AN09B017g (ascending relay neurons)
   THE SELECTIVITY SPECTRUM
   - AN09B017b: ppk23-selective (12.6:1 ratio)
   - AN09B017g: ppk25-selective (0.06 ratio = 16:1 for ppk25)
   - Each targets different mAL_m subsets (Panel K)
   - Story: "These relay neurons sit at opposite ends of the pheromone
     selectivity spectrum. AN09B017b routes male contact (ppk23) and
     AN09B017g routes female contact (ppk25) to distinct mAL_m subsets.
     The relay layer is where pheromone discrimination begins -- before
     the male-specific mAL population performs its own computations."

Note: mAL4F (the most ppk23-inhibited subtype in the full analysis) is
a shared type and is excluded from this male-specific subset. The sign
reversal phenomenon is still present within the mAL_m population but
the most extreme example is not visible here.


ONE-SENTENCE FIGURE CAPTION
============================
"The 16 male-specific mAL subtypes alone encode pheromone encounter
identity through differential relay routing, sign-reversing inhibition,
and lateral competition, delivering channel-specific gating of P1
courtship command neurons."
