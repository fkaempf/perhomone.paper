Figure 6 — Multimodal Sensory Integration in the Male Drosophila Courtship Circuit
====================================================================================
Narrative summary and key takeaways


THE QUESTION
------------
Male courtship in Drosophila requires the fly to evaluate multiple sensory
cues simultaneously: pheromones detected by olfactory receptor neurons
(three glomerular channels: DA1, VA1v, VA1d), courtship song detected by
auditory neurons (JO-B), visual motion detected by lobula columnar neurons
(LC10), and contact chemosensory cues detected by gustatory-like neurons
expressing ppk23 and ppk25. That is seven distinct sensory channels.

How does the male brain wire these channels together so that the right
combination of cues activates courtship command neurons — and does the
wiring merely pool information, or does it actively compute over
combinations?


THE CIRCUIT
-----------
Using the male CNS connectome we traced all synaptic paths (up to 3-4
hops) from each of the seven sensory populations to four groups of
downstream decision neurons:

  - aSP-f    8 unique target neuron types, 12,400 paths traced
  - aSP-g    5 unique target neuron types,  7,750 paths traced
  - vAB3    13 unique target neuron types, 247,000 paths evaluated
  - PPN1    13 unique target neuron types, 247,000 paths evaluated

  Total: 36 unique target neuron types across all sets.

The circuit has a characteristic three-layer architecture:

  Sensory neurons  -->  2nd-order relay/interneurons  -->  3rd-order targets

Between the sensory layer and the targets sit hundreds of intermediate
neuron types that appear in paths from one or more modalities. These are
not passive relays; they are where integration happens.


==============================================================================
PART I: THE WIRING — WHO GETS WHAT, AND WITH WHAT SIGN (Panels A-C)
==============================================================================

Panel A: Circuit schematic.
  Seven modalities converging through 2nd-order relays onto 3rd-order
  targets. The diagram lays out the basic feed-forward architecture:
  olfactory (DA1, VA1v, VA1d) sensory neurons connect through labeled-line
  projection neurons; auditory (JO-B) and visual (LC10) route through
  their own relays; ppk23/ppk25 contact neurons reach targets through
  leg-nerve projection neurons (LgLG1a, LgLG1b, LgLG5-8 families) and
  antennal interneurons (AN09B017, AN05B series).

Panel B: Modality composition heatmap — "Who gets what."
  Rows = target neurons, columns = 7 modalities, color = total path
  strength (column-normalized so each modality's maximum = 1).

  Not all modalities contribute equally in absolute terms. Across all
  36 targets, mean path strengths per modality are:

    ppk23    0.058     (strongest — dominates 21 of 36 targets)
    ppk25    0.056
    DA1      0.030
    VA1v     0.010
    VA1d     0.005
    visual   0.003
    auditory 0.001     (weakest overall)

  Contact channels (ppk23+ppk25) carry roughly 70% of total path
  strength across all targets. DA1 carries ~19%. VA1v and VA1d together
  ~9%. Auditory and visual are comparatively minor contributors in raw
  strength, though they reach nearly every target at lower levels.

  But column-normalization reveals that every modality has its own
  pattern of relative targeting: even auditory, the weakest channel in
  absolute terms, shows structure — it targets some neurons more than
  others, and this pattern is not random.

Panel C: Excitatory/inhibitory valence heatmap — "With what sign."
  Same structure as Panel B, but split into excitatory and inhibitory
  contributions, then combined as a diverging net-valence map.

  Modalities differ dramatically in their E/I balance:

  Predominantly excitatory (>70% excitatory):
    DA1       ~89% excitatory across all targets
    visual    ~79% excitatory
    ppk23     ~63% excitatory
    ppk25     ~66% excitatory

  Predominantly inhibitory:
    VA1v      ~80% inhibitory (!) — the VA1v pheromone channel reaches
              targets primarily through inhibitory paths
    VA1d      ~50/50 excitatory/inhibitory overall

  The VA1v result is striking: this glomerulus, activated by fly odors
  including pheromones, delivers mostly inhibition to courtship targets.
  At aSP-f targets: 84% of VA1v path strength is inhibitory. At aSP-g:
  87% inhibitory. This is not a minor effect — VA1v is the 2nd strongest
  olfactory input to aSP-f targets (18% of total strength) and nearly
  all of it arrives as inhibition.

  VA1d is balanced (~50/50) but this varies sharply by target set:
  at aSP-f targets it is 73% excitatory, at PPN1 targets it is 80%
  inhibitory. The same glomerulus flips valence depending on which
  downstream population it reaches.

  These E/I signatures mean the circuit doesn't just deliver "how much"
  of each modality — it delivers "how much excitation" and "how much
  inhibition" separately, enabling signed computation downstream.


==============================================================================
PART II: HOW MULTIMODAL ARE THE TARGETS? (Panel D)
==============================================================================

Shannon entropy and lifetime sparseness quantify how broadly each target
samples across the seven modalities.

  Across all 36 targets:
    Median entropy:              1.60 bits (of 2.81 maximum)
    Mean entropy:                1.72 bits
    Range:                       1.21 – 2.49 bits
    Median effective modalities: 3.0 (= 2^entropy)
    Median breadth:              4 modalities (>5% of max input)
    Median dominance:            48% of input from top modality

  No target receives input from only one modality. The least multimodal
  neurons (e.g., mAL_m1, H=1.34; AVLP606, H=1.21) still receive non-
  trivial input from 2-3 channels. The most multimodal (LH006m, H=2.49;
  SLP160, H=2.46; AVLP753m, H=2.40) sample broadly from 5-6 modalities
  with no single modality exceeding ~30% of total input.

  The distributions differ by target set:

    aSP-f:  median H = 2.23, breadth = 6. The most multimodal set.
            Dominant modality: DA1 (4/8 neurons), but with 41% dominance
            — meaning even in DA1-dominated neurons, 59% of input comes
            from other channels.

    aSP-g:  median H = 1.70, breadth = 4.
            Dominant modality: ppk23 (all 5 neurons). Contact channels
            dominate but not exclusively — olfactory channels contribute
            a non-trivial minority.

    vAB3:   median H = 1.55, breadth = 4. Mixed dominance: ppk23 (7/13),
            ppk25 (4/13), DA1 (2/13).

    PPN1:   median H = 1.45, breadth = 3. The most concentrated set.
            ppk23 dominates 9/13 targets, ppk25 dominates 3/13.

  The key observation: aSP-f targets are the most broadly multimodal,
  integrating olfactory, contact, visual and other inputs. PPN1 targets
  are the most narrowly focused on contact channels. aSP-g and vAB3 are
  intermediate. This means different output populations are wired to
  sample different widths of the sensory space.


==============================================================================
PART III: WHICH MODALITIES CO-TRAVEL? (Panel E)
==============================================================================

This is the core modularity question: when two modalities co-target the
same neurons, they have the opportunity to interact. Modalities that
rarely co-target will remain functionally independent regardless of
circuit depth.

Four complementary metrics reveal the co-targeting structure:

PEARSON CORRELATION of modality strength vectors across all 36 targets:

              DA1   VA1v   VA1d   aud   vis   ppk23  ppk25
  DA1        1.00   0.76   0.48  -0.11  0.13   0.09   0.24
  VA1v       0.76   1.00   0.51  -0.11  0.31  -0.08   0.05
  VA1d       0.48   0.51   1.00  -0.08  0.12  -0.06   0.03
  auditory  -0.11  -0.11  -0.08   1.00 -0.14   0.04   0.09
  visual     0.13   0.31   0.12  -0.14  1.00  -0.05   0.00
  ppk23      0.09  -0.08  -0.06   0.04 -0.05   1.00   0.96
  ppk25      0.24   0.05   0.03   0.09  0.00   0.96   1.00

Three modality blocs emerge:

  1. THE OLFACTORY TRIO (DA1, VA1v, VA1d)
     Strongly correlated: DA1-VA1v r = 0.76, DA1-VA1d r = 0.48,
     VA1v-VA1d r = 0.51. These three pheromone channels co-target the
     same neurons — they travel together through the circuit.

     Spearman (rank-based) correlations are even stronger: DA1-VA1v
     rho = 0.92, VA1v-VA1d rho = 0.85, DA1-VA1d rho = 0.81.

     This isn't because they share sensory neurons (they are distinct
     glomeruli) — it is because the circuit routes them through
     overlapping downstream intermediates. DA1_lPN (the DA1 projection
     neuron) is the single strongest intermediate node overall (total
     strength 0.85) and carries 94% DA1 signal, but also 4% VA1v and
     3% VA1d. M_lvPNm45 is similar (91% DA1, 4% VA1v, 3% VA1d).
     VA1v_vPN carries 90% VA1v but also 7% DA1. VA1d_adPN carries 88%
     VA1d but also 6% VA1v and 6% DA1.

     The olfactory trio shares intermediate neurons because their
     labeled-line projection neurons project to overlapping lateral horn
     and AVLP targets. By the time signal reaches layer 3, the three
     olfactory channels are partially mixed.

  2. THE CONTACT PAIR (ppk23, ppk25)
     The tightest correlation in the dataset: Pearson r = 0.96, Spearman
     rho = 0.98. These two channels are nearly interchangeable in their
     targeting pattern — they reach the same neurons with almost
     identical relative strengths.

     They are also anticorrelated or uncorrelated with the olfactory
     trio: ppk23-DA1 r = 0.09, ppk23-VA1v r = -0.08, ppk25-VA1d
     r = 0.03. Contact and olfactory channels target different neurons.

     Despite their tight co-targeting, ppk23 and ppk25 are NOT
     identical. They enter the circuit through different sensory neuron
     families (ppk23: LgLG1a, LgLG6, LgLG7, WG4; ppk25: LgLG1b,
     LgLG5, LgLG8, WG3) and these route through the AN09B017 family
     with different relative weights. AN09B017a is ppk23-preferring
     (66% ppk23), AN09B017g is ppk25-preferring (72% ppk25), and
     AN09B017f is roughly balanced (45%/55%). This creates the diversity
     that later enables different interaction signs at different targets.

  3. AUDITORY AND VISUAL: THE LONERS
     Auditory is weakly anticorrelated with everything: r = -0.11 with
     DA1, -0.11 with VA1v, -0.08 with VA1d, -0.14 with visual, +0.04
     with ppk23, +0.09 with ppk25. It is the most independent channel.

     Visual is weakly positive with VA1v (r = 0.31) and weakly negative
     or uncorrelated with everything else. It routes through the largest
     number of unique intermediate neurons (3,701 — vs ~1,400 for
     olfactory and contact channels) but at low per-path strength.

     Despite their independence, auditory and visual do reach 34 of 36
     targets (all 7 modalities appear at the most multimodal neurons).
     They are present but at low amplitude.

CO-ROUTING THROUGH SHARED INTERMEDIATES (Jaccard similarity):

  Which modality pairs physically share the same relay neurons?

              DA1   VA1v   VA1d   aud   vis   ppk23  ppk25
  DA1        1.00   0.60   0.59  0.25  0.25   0.34   0.34
  VA1v       0.60   1.00   0.72  0.27  0.25   0.35   0.36
  VA1d       0.59   0.72   1.00  0.25  0.25   0.34   0.35
  auditory   0.25   0.27   0.25  1.00  0.18   0.27   0.28
  visual     0.25   0.25   0.25  0.18  1.00   0.25   0.24
  ppk23      0.34   0.35   0.34  0.27  0.25   1.00   0.75
  ppk25      0.34   0.36   0.35  0.28  0.24   0.75   1.00

  The same three blocs are visible. The olfactory trio shares 60-72%
  of intermediate neurons with each other. ppk23 and ppk25 share 75%.
  Cross-bloc sharing is 25-36% — present but lower.

  The number of shared intermediate neurons:
    DA1-VA1v: 1,096 shared intermediates
    DA1-VA1d: 1,114
    VA1v-VA1d: 1,243 (highest olfactory pair — these two share the
               most relay infrastructure)
    ppk23-ppk25: 1,185
    DA1-ppk23: 722 (cross-bloc)
    Auditory-visual: 721 (the two loners share surprisingly many
               intermediates with each other — but at low total strength)

MODALITY CORRELATIONS DIFFER BY TARGET SET:

  The co-targeting structure is not uniform — it shifts depending on
  which targets you examine:

  At aSP-f targets: DA1-ppk25 r = 0.82, DA1-ppk23 r = 0.57 — the
  olfactory and contact channels are POSITIVELY correlated here. Also
  auditory-visual r = 0.62 — these two loners co-target aSP-f neurons.

  At aSP-g targets: VA1v-visual r = 0.69, ppk23-visual r = 0.79 —
  visual joins the contact cluster. DA1-VA1v r = 0.85 — olfactory
  trio remains tight. Auditory is anticorrelated with olfactory
  (auditory-VA1d r = -0.94).

  At vAB3 targets: olfactory and contact are anticorrelated or
  uncorrelated (ppk23-VA1v r = -0.30, ppk23-DA1 r = 0.02). The two
  blocs target different vAB3 neurons.

  At PPN1 targets: all three olfactory channels plus visual are
  strongly positively correlated (DA1-VA1d r = 0.93, VA1v-visual
  r = 0.85). Contact channels correlate with each other (r = 0.92)
  but not with olfactory.

  These target-set-specific patterns mean the circuit implements
  different co-targeting logic at different output populations.


==============================================================================
PART IV: HOW MANY ROUTES? PATH DIVERSITY (Panel F)
==============================================================================

For each (target, modality) pair we rank paths by strength and count how
many paths are needed to accumulate 80% of total strength. Concentrated
routing = few paths carry most of the signal; distributed routing = signal
spreads across many parallel paths.

  Median paths to 80% by modality (across all targets):
    DA1       44
    VA1v      87
    VA1d     136
    ppk23    120
    ppk25    116
    auditory 338
    visual   686

  DA1 is the most concentrated: a few strong projection neurons (DA1_lPN,
  M_lvPNm45) carry the bulk of the signal. At aSP-f targets, the median
  is just 6.5 paths to 80% — nearly all DA1 input arrives through a tiny
  handful of routes.

  Visual is the most distributed: median 686 paths needed, reflecting its
  routing through >3,700 unique intermediate neurons at low per-path
  strength. This is not "noise" — it represents massively parallel,
  redundant routing that makes visual input robust to loss of any single
  path.

  Auditory is intermediate in total strength but also quite distributed
  (338 paths), reflecting its weak but broad connectivity.

  Path diversity varies by target set:

                DA1    VA1d   VA1v   aud    ppk23  ppk25  visual
    aSP-f       6.5    13     17     28.5   116.5  91.5    65.5
    aSP-g      27      28     11     28     104    131      88
    vAB3       77     264    134    405     336    221     899
    PPN1      215     236    190    379      73     52    1612

  At aSP-f targets, DA1 funnels through just ~7 paths — these are the
  classic labeled-line olfactory projection neurons. At PPN1 targets,
  DA1 needs ~215 paths — the routing is far more distributed.

  The contrast between concentrated (aSP-f) and distributed (PPN1/vAB3)
  routing reveals a design principle: aSP-f is wired for high-fidelity,
  few-path input from olfaction; vAB3/PPN1 are wired for low-fidelity,
  many-path input that is more robust but noisier.

PART V: WHERE INTEGRATION HAPPENS — THE HUBS (Panels G-H)
==============================================================================

We parsed all paths, extracted every intermediate node (positions 2
through N-1), and scored each by total path strength and modality
breadth.

THE ALL-7-MODALITY HUB NEURONS (appear in paths from every channel):

  Rank  Neuron         Strength   Dominant     Profile
  ────  ─────────────  ─────────  ──────────── ─────────────────────────
   1    DA1_lPN        0.852      DA1 (94%)    Olfactory labeled-line
   2    AN09B017g      0.804      ppk25 (72%)  Contact-biased hub
   3    AN09B017f      0.745      ppk25 (55%)  Contact-biased hub
   4    AN09B017e      0.484      ppk23 (58%)  Contact-biased hub
   5    AN09B017a      0.361      ppk23 (66%)  Contact-biased hub
   6    M_lvPNm45      0.279      DA1 (91%)    Olfactory labeled-line
   7    AN09B017b      0.265      ppk23 (73%)  Contact-biased hub
   8    LH008m         0.183      DA1 (73%)    Olfactory-biased integrator
   9    mAL_m1         0.164      ppk23 (56%)  GABAergic convergence hub
  10    mAL_m3c        0.097      ppk23 (47%)  GABAergic convergence hub
  11    SIP101m        0.086      —             Broad integrator
  12    mAL_m5a        0.071      ppk23 (52%)  GABAergic convergence hub
  13    P1_3c          0.066      ppk25 (47%)  Known courtship neuron

  All 7 AN09B017 family members reach paths from every modality, but
  their modality profiles are very different:

    AN09B017a:  66% ppk23, 34% ppk25  (ppk23-preferring)
    AN09B017b:  73% ppk23, 27% ppk25  (strongly ppk23-preferring)
    AN09B017d:  60% ppk23, 40% ppk25
    AN09B017e:  58% ppk23, 40% ppk25 + 1% DA1
    AN09B017f:  45% ppk23, 55% ppk25  (weakly ppk25-preferring)
    AN09B017g:  28% ppk23, 72% ppk25  (strongly ppk25-preferring)

  Despite being classified as "7-modality" hubs, the AN09B017 neurons
  carry overwhelmingly contact-channel signal (>95% from ppk23+ppk25).
  Their "all-7" status reflects the fact that olfactory, auditory, and
  visual signals also pass through them, but at very low relative
  strength. Their primary computational role is as contact-channel
  relay/splitters.

  The olfactory-biased hubs (DA1_lPN, M_lvPNm45, VA1v_vPN, VA1d_adPN)
  are the classic labeled-line projection neurons — they carry 88-94%
  of signal from a single glomerulus but also pick up small amounts of
  other modalities via recurrent connections.

  The genuine multimodal integrators — neurons where no single modality
  exceeds ~50% — are:

    P1_3c:    8% DA1, 3% VA1v, 40% ppk23, 47% ppk25, 1% vis, 0.2% aud
    LH008m:   73% DA1, 12% VA1v, 3% VA1d, 9% ppk25, 3% ppk23
    mAL_m3c:  15% DA1, 47% ppk23, 37% ppk25

  LH008m is notable: it is the highest-ranking node that carries
  substantial olfactory (DA1 + VA1v = 85%) AND contact (ppk23 + ppk25
  = 12%) input. It is a genuine cross-bloc integrator, one of the
  rare points where olfactory and contact streams physically converge
  at the intermediate layer.

THE GABAergic SIGN-INVERTING HUBS:

  mAL_m1 (strength 0.164, all 7 modalities, 56% ppk23):
    Receives excitatory convergent input from AN09B017 family, then
    delivers GABAergic inhibition downstream. All 121 presynaptic
    partners feed it excitatory AN09B017 signal; it inverts this to
    inhibition when projecting to targets like LH008m, mAL_m2a.

  mAL_m3c, mAL_m5a, mAL_m4, mAL_m5c:
    Same role — receive multimodal excitatory input, deliver inhibition.
    Together with mAL_m1, these create a battery of sign-inverting hubs
    that give downstream targets access to "negative copies" of the
    sensory signals.

  AN05B035 (strength 0.316, 6 modalities, 57% ppk23):
    GABAergic interneuron that provides strong inhibition to AN09B017g
    (weight -0.22). This is the key node that makes AN09B017g carry
    ppk23 signal with NEGATIVE sign — AN05B035 receives ppk23 sensory
    input and inhibits AN09B017g, so AN09B017g's ppk23 activation is
    actually negative (-0.038 at step 2). This is the origin of
    push-pull topology in the contact system.

PART VI: aSP-f vs aSP-g — TWO POPULATIONS, TWO SENSORY WORLDS (Panels I-J)
==============================================================================

aSP-f and aSP-g are both male-specific courtship command neurons, but
the connectome reveals they sample completely different slices of the
sensory input space.

  Modality composition (% of total path strength):

                aSP-f     aSP-g
    DA1         49.5%      9.2%     (5.4x stronger at aSP-f)
    VA1v        18.1%      2.5%     (7.2x stronger at aSP-f)
    VA1d         9.3%      0.7%     (13x stronger at aSP-f)
    ppk23        7.2%     45.9%     (6.4x stronger at aSP-g)
    ppk25       10.9%     39.7%     (3.6x stronger at aSP-g)
    visual       4.6%      1.4%     (3.3x stronger at aSP-f)
    auditory     0.3%      0.6%     (comparable)

    Olfactory total:   76.9%    12.4%
    Contact total:     18.1%    85.7%

  aSP-f is an olfactory-dominated integrator: three-quarters of its
  input is pheromonal (DA1 + VA1v + VA1d). Contact, visual, and
  auditory channels provide the remaining quarter.

  aSP-g is a contact-dominated integrator: 86% of its input comes from
  ppk23 and ppk25. Olfaction provides ~12%, visual and auditory ~2%.

  This is a dramatic segregation. Despite being downstream targets in
  the "same" courtship circuit, aSP-f and aSP-g live in different
  sensory worlds. aSP-f is primarily asking "what does the fly smell?"
  while aSP-g is primarily asking "what is the fly touching?"

  The E/I valence patterns differ too:

    At aSP-f targets:
      DA1 is 93% excitatory — strongly drives aSP-f.
      VA1v is 84% inhibitory — actively suppresses aSP-f.
      VA1d is 73% excitatory.
      ppk25 is 78% excitatory; ppk23 is 55%/45% balanced.
      Visual is 94% excitatory.

    At aSP-g targets:
      DA1 is 84% excitatory (weaker but still excitatory).
      VA1v is 87% inhibitory (same sign as at aSP-f).
      VA1d is 62% inhibitory (flipped vs aSP-f where it is excitatory).
      ppk23 is 58%/42% balanced; ppk25 is 60%/40%.
      Auditory is 77% excitatory.

  VA1v is consistently inhibitory at both target populations — it is
  a "braking" channel that suppresses courtship targets regardless of
  which population. The excitatory DA1 and inhibitory VA1v create an
  opponent olfactory motif: DA1 (cVA-activated) pushes toward courtship,
  VA1v (broadly fly-odor-activated) pulls away. This opponent coding
  may help distinguish conspecific female pheromone from non-specific
  fly odors.


==============================================================================
PART VII: INTEGRATION BUILDS WITH CIRCUIT DEPTH (Panel K)
==============================================================================

Assigning each neuron in a path a circuit order (1 = sensory, 2 = first
relay, 3 = second relay, etc.) and computing modality breadth at each
layer reveals progressive convergence:

  Layer 1 (sensory neurons): Unimodal by definition.
  Layer 2 (first-order relays): Mostly unimodal. Olfactory projection
    neurons carry one glomerulus. Contact relay neurons (LgLG family)
    carry one ppk channel. Cross-modal mixing is minimal.
  Layer 3 (second-order relays): Increasing multimodality. ~3-4 modalities
    per neuron. The olfactory trio begins to merge here (DA1+VA1v+VA1d
    co-target the same layer-3 neurons). Contact channels merge with
    each other but remain largely separate from olfactory.
  Layer 4+ (targets): Most neurons receive 4-6 modalities. True cross-
    bloc integration (olfactory + contact + visual) emerges here.

  The convergence is NOT a step function — it builds gradually. And the
  MODULE STRUCTURE persists: even at layer 4, olfactory channels are
  more correlated with each other than with contact channels. The
  circuit preserves within-bloc correlations while allowing cross-bloc
  conjunctions only at the deepest layers.


==============================================================================
PART VIII: COMBINATORIAL SIGNAL FLOW — THE PUNCHLINE (Panels L-Q)
==============================================================================

Everything above describes the wiring. The combinatorial signal flow
analysis asks: does this wiring actually produce nonlinear computation?

Method: Propagate signal through the signed connectome using saturating
activation functions. Test all 127 combinations of 7 channels (singles,
pairs, triplets, ..., all 7 together). Compute interaction scores:

    R_ij = A(i+j) - A(i) - A(j)
  Positive R_ij = synergy (super-additive: combined > sum of singles)
  Negative R_ij = opposition (sub-additive: combined < sum of singles)

The deep investigation focused on the ppk23 x ppk25 pair because these
two contact channels have the highest co-targeting correlation (r = 0.96)
yet show dramatically different interaction signs at different targets.

Key destructive targets (R_ij < 0):
  LH006m (R ~ -0.26), LH003m, AVLP700m (-0.30), AVLP704m (-0.26),
  mAL_m3c, and ALL PPN1 downstream targets (uniformly destructive).

Key super-additive targets (R_ij > 0):
  LH008m (+0.18), LH001m, AVLP750m (+0.17), mAL_m2a (+0.23),
  AVLP728m (+0.08).

FOUR MECHANISMS determine the interaction sign:

1. CEILING SATURATION (destructive, R_ij < 0)
   Both ppk23 and ppk25 deliver same-sign excitatory drive. Combined
   input overshoots the linear regime.
   Examples: LH006m, AVLP700m, AVLP704m, all PPN1 targets.

2. FLOOR SATURATION (super-additive, R_ij > 0)
   Both channels deliver same-sign inhibitory drive. Sublinear
   inhibition.
   Example: AVLP750m (ppk23 = -0.009, ppk25 = -0.003, both inhibit).

3. PUSH-PULL (super-additive, R_ij > 0)
   Channels deliver opposite-sign drives: one excites, one inhibits.
   The inhibitory channel rescues the excitatory one from saturation.
   Examples: LH008m (ppk23 inhibits via AN09B017g -> mAL_m1 path,
   ppk25 excites via AN09B017g direct), mAL_m2a.

4. UPSTREAM SATURATION (destructive despite push-pull topology)
   Intermediary neurons are themselves co-saturated. Their R_ij is
   already negative, and this upstream compression propagates downstream.
   Examples: mAL_m1, mAL_m9, AVLP727m, AVLP743m.

The interaction sign is robust across all tested nonlinearities (ReLU,
leaky ReLU, sigmoid at various betas). Sign concordance >80%.

The qualitative interaction landscape is a property of the wiring, not
an artifact of the model.


==============================================================================
KEY TAKEAWAYS
==============================================================================

1. The courtship circuit is genuinely multimodal, not pheromone-dominated.
   Across all 36 targets, the median neuron receives input from 4
   modalities and no modality exceeds 48% of total input. Even the
   narrowest targets (PPN1 downstream) sample from 3+ channels.

2. Two dominant sensory blocs emerge: olfactory (DA1-VA1v-VA1d) and
   contact (ppk23-ppk25). These travel through the circuit in parallel
   modules. Within each bloc, channels are tightly correlated
   (olfactory: rho = 0.81-0.92; contact: r = 0.96). Between blocs,
   correlations are near zero or negative. Auditory and visual route
   independently at low strength but broad coverage.

3. VA1v is a "braking" pheromone channel. Despite being olfactory, 80%+
   of VA1v path strength is inhibitory at courtship targets. Together
   with the excitatory DA1 channel, this creates opponent olfactory
   coding: DA1 drives courtship, VA1v suppresses it.

4. aSP-f and aSP-g sample different sensory worlds. aSP-f is 77%
   olfactory, 18% contact. aSP-g is 86% contact, 12% olfactory. They
   are not redundant command neurons — they evaluate different aspects
   of the multimodal scene.

5. Modality co-targeting is target-set-specific. Olfactory and contact
   channels are positively correlated at aSP-f targets but uncorrelated
   or anticorrelated at vAB3 targets. The same circuit implements
   different co-targeting logic for different output populations.

6. The AN09B017 projection neuron family is a contact-channel
   splitter/router. Seven variants carry ppk23 and ppk25 with different
   ratios (from 73% ppk23 at AN09B017b to 72% ppk25 at AN09B017g),
   creating sign-diversified copies available to downstream targets.
   They also carry trace olfactory and other signals, linking them to
   all 7 modalities.

7. mAL inhibitory neurons are confirmed as central sign-inverting
   hubs. GABAergic mAL neurons (m1, m3c, m4, m5a, m5c) receive
   multimodal excitatory convergence and deliver inhibition downstream,
   enabling push-pull integration motifs.

8. Path diversity reveals routing strategies. DA1 funnels through ~7
   paths at aSP-f (high-fidelity labeled-line). Visual distributes
   across ~686 paths (low per-path strength, high redundancy). Contact
   channels are intermediate (~116 paths). Different modalities use
   different routing strategies to reach the same targets.

9. Integration builds progressively with circuit depth. Layer 2 neurons
   are mostly unimodal. Layer 3 merges within-bloc modalities (the
   olfactory trio converges). Layer 4+ enables cross-bloc integration.
   The circuit is not a single mixing step — it is a staged pipeline.

10. The circuit computes, not just converges. Combinatorial signal flow
    reveals genuine nonlinear interactions: specific modality pairs are
    synergistic (push-pull de-saturation), others are destructive
    (ceiling saturation), and the pattern is target-specific and robust
    across model variants. Different output populations extract
    different nonlinear features from the same sensory input space.


==============================================================================
WHAT WE HAVE LEARNED
==============================================================================

The traditional view casts the male courtship circuit as a pheromone-
driven labeled-line system: cVA -> DA1 ORNs -> projection neurons ->
P1/aSP-f/aSP-g command neurons -> courtship. This analysis reveals that
picture is at best one subplot.

The courtship circuit is a structured multimodal integration device. It
receives seven parallel sensory streams organized into two major blocs
(olfactory and contact) plus two independent channels (auditory, visual).
These streams are kept modular through the first relay layers, preserving
within-bloc correlations. They are progressively merged at deeper layers
through a compact set of intermediate hubs — the AN09B017 contact-channel
splitters, the mAL sign-inverting interneurons, the labeled-line
olfactory projection neurons — that actively reshape, split, and invert
signals before delivering them to targets.

Different output populations sample different slices of this sensory
space. aSP-f is olfactory-dominated and broadly multimodal. aSP-g is
contact-dominated and narrower. PPN1 downstream is the most contact-
focused and shows uniformly destructive (sub-additive) interactions.
vAB3 downstream is the most heterogeneous, with individual targets
spanning the full range from strongly synergistic to strongly opposed.

The excitatory/inhibitory architecture is not decoration — it is
computational infrastructure. VA1v's predominantly inhibitory routing
creates an olfactory opponent channel. The mAL neurons' sign inversion
enables push-pull motifs. The AN09B017 family's channel-preference
diversity enables different targets to receive the same contact signals
with different signs. These wiring features produce genuine nonlinear
interactions that are robust to the specific choice of activation
function.

The picture that emerges is one of a circuit that has been shaped by
evolution to do more than sum up sensory evidence. It selectively
amplifies specific combinations of cues, suppresses others, and routes
different multimodal features to different output populations — a
structured computational device for courtship decision-making.
